A little about
my journey

I chose Geodetic and Geomatics Engineering because the field sits at an interesting intersection — broad enough to be applied across many sectors, yet grounded in a clear, technical discipline. What I didn't fully anticipate was how much of my work, both academic and professional, would end up revolving around spatial data.

Over time, I found myself drawn to GIS specifically. There's something compelling about how it can take information that's otherwise difficult to interpret — hazard zones, demographic patterns, infrastructure gaps — and make it spatially visible and actionable. That shift from raw data to something people can actually read and respond to is what I find genuinely interesting.

My involvement in disaster risk mapping projects, particularly at the detail level of 1:5,000-scale assessments, helped me develop a more practical understanding of spatial analysis workflows, data integration, and the care that goes into communicating geospatial information clearly.

Studying as an exchange student at Universiti Teknologi Malaysia, and participating in international exhibition events in Malaysia and South Korea, pushed me to adapt quickly to different environments and work alongside people with different approaches. Those experiences shaped how I think about learning — as something continuous, not something that ends with a degree.

My direction from here is toward GIS analysis, while also building a stronger foundation in geospatial databases and data analysis. I'm interested in how these tools can work together to support better, more informed decisions.

I see this portfolio not as a showcase of everything I already know, but as a reflection of what I have learned so far — and where I hope to grow next.
